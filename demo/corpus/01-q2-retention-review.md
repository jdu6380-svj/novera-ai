# Asteria Workcloud — Q2 2026 Customer Retention Review

## Scope and method

This review covers the fictional RelayOS trial-to-paid funnel for January through June 2026. It combines product events, cancellation reasons, and coded support conversations. The review is intended to guide product decisions, not to establish a causal model.

The denominator for activation is new trial workspaces with at least two invited teammates. Retention is measured at the workspace level. A workspace can have more than one support ticket, so coded reason percentages describe mentions rather than unique customers.

## Executive summary

The April 2026 trial-to-paid cohort reached 84% 90-day paid-logo retention, compared with a 91% average for the January–March 2026 cohorts.

First-week activation fell from 68% in the January–March 2026 baseline to 52% in the April–June 2026 cohort.

The largest observed friction is early setup: new workspaces struggle to publish a first workflow and connect an integration before they understand RelayOS terminology. The data supports prioritizing onboarding clarity and measuring the result with activation and retention guardrails.

## Activation funnel

The activation event is “first workflow published within 48 hours of workspace creation.” It is a leading indicator, not a retention substitute. In the April–June cohort, 52% of eligible workspaces reached the event, down from 68% in the January–March baseline.

Workspaces that completed the event generally invited a teammate and connected at least one integration during the first session. Workspaces that stopped at role mapping or integration setup generated more help requests and were less likely to return in the following week.

## Churn evidence

### Coded reasons

Among 126 coded churn interviews and support tickets, 44% cited onboarding setup confusion, 31% cited delayed integration setup, 15% cited the 10-seat minimum, and 10% cited other reasons.

The coding pass used one primary reason per conversation. “Onboarding setup confusion” includes uncertainty about roles, the first workflow, and what to do after inviting a teammate.

### Segment pattern

Trial workspaces that did not publish a first workflow within 48 hours were 3.2 times more likely to cancel within 90 days than workspaces that did.

This segment comparison is directional. It controls for the activation event but not for company size, sales-assisted onboarding, or pre-existing integration complexity.

## Interpretation and limits

The retention review shows a strong sequence of signals: activation declined, setup confusion was the most common coded reason, and delayed first-workflow publication was associated with cancellation. It does not prove that the April release caused every cancellation.

The review does not contain revenue, gross-retention, or account-level personal data. Any answer about ARR, contract value, or an individual customer is outside the evidence in this document.

## Approved next actions

Product and Customer Success approved a two-week onboarding experiment before changing the default flow for every workspace. The experiment brief is the source of truth for its sample, treatment, and guardrails.
